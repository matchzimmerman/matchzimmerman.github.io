# Behavior as Narrative

## Core proposition

The work is not an abstract animation with a soundtrack. It is a hidden dynamic system encountered through image and sound.

The artwork exists in the relationship between:

1. a rule-based field whose full operation remains concealed; and
2. a human nervous system attempting to perceive, predict, and understand it.

Narrative does not require characters, dialogue, or represented events. It can emerge from changes in expectation: agreement, hesitation, withholding, recognition, loss, fracture, recurrence, and release.

## Sonic Visuals

Sound and image should not be treated as separate layers. They should emerge from the same oscillators, coupling values, memory, drift, phase, and interference.

This is not audio-reactive visualization. It is one phenomenon made perceptible through two senses.

When visible vectors synchronize, harmonic relationships should tighten. When the field fractures, the sonic relationship should fracture. When an earlier visual structure returns as residue, its acoustic trace should return as memory rather than as a literal repeated cue.

## Perceptual threshold

The strongest state is the threshold between noise and meaning.

The system should allow the viewer to almost recognize a structure without fully resolving it. Temporary forms can resemble braids, tunnels, maps, crystals, volumes, faces, or impossible geometry, but the generator must not switch to a separate illustrative representation.

Recognition is an event in the viewer, not an object delivered by the system.

## Behavioral vocabulary

These terms describe effects on attention rather than visual styles:

- **entraining** — repeated movement begins to synchronize the viewer's attention;
- **searching** — local relationships change without reaching stable agreement;
- **withholding** — coherence approaches resolution but refuses to complete;
- **locking** — independent rhythms enter sudden temporary consensus;
- **fracturing** — established agreement propagates into disagreement;
- **haunting** — an earlier structure reappears as residue or memory;
- **misleading** — the field suggests one resolution and becomes another;
- **recovering** — coherence gradually returns after collapse;
- **dissolving** — a legible relationship becomes distributed motion again.

This vocabulary can function as a movement board for composing longer works. Clips and live passages are organized by changes in behavior rather than by depicted scenes.

## Instrument grammar

1. Never illustrate; only allow.
2. Rotation is a primitive, not a decorative effect.
3. Rhythm is more important than geometry.
4. Relationships carry more meaning than positions.
5. No state should feel fully finished or completely explained.
6. Recurrence should be recognizable but never identical.
7. Persistent memory should visibly and sonically influence the future.
8. Orange is semantic: station identity, calibration, and rare anomalies.
9. `FIELD STATION: MAGPIE` remains a fixed amber phosphor identity.
10. CRT behavior is part of transmission and observation, not a retro filter.

## Research question

Can a procedural audiovisual system produce the felt structure of narrative without conventional representation?

Related questions:

- What minimum sensory conditions create anticipation, conflict, transformation, memory, and release?
- How does temporary synchronization affect attention and embodied expectation?
- Can sound and image generated by shared dynamics be perceived as one event rather than synchronized media?
- Can an adaptive system cultivate sustained attention without optimizing for compulsive engagement?

## Ethical direction

A future adaptive version should not optimize for time-on-screen, addiction, or escalation. It should support depth of encounter.

The system may reward patience with subtlety, quietness, delayed recurrence, and states that require sustained perception. The viewer studies the field while the field gradually learns how its behaviors are encountered, without collapsing that relationship into conventional engagement metrics.

## Archive model

Each output is a field sample from an ongoing instrument, not a separate disconnected artwork.

A record may include:

- inherited state and parent execution;
- rotational frequencies;
- coupling and memory values;
- periods of consensus and fracture;
- emergent structures observed but not explicitly rendered;
- sonic changes derived from the same field;
- a concise interpretive log.

Over time, recurring behaviors and notable events can acquire names: the long braid, the silent fracture, the false lock, the field that remembered itself. The archive becomes the observational history of a synthetic phenomenon.
