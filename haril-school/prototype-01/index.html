Learning patterns

How Maya tends to work.

These are provisional patterns derived from actual process evidence. They are editable by the student and should never become permanent labels.

Strong entry mode

Visual observation and collecting often generate stronger initial questions than abstract prompts.

Useful scaffold

An intermediate constraint — sketch, map, three-source limit, prototype — improves completion without suppressing exploration.

Recurring stall

Large open-ended writing tasks tend to trigger delay. Oral explanation or diagramming often unlocks the first draft.

Revision behavior

Maya revises more deeply when critique happens mid-process and is framed around questions rather than correctness.

Self-model mismatch

“I don't like science” conflicts with high engagement in ecology, sensing and spatial data when those domains connect to place.

Emerging strength

Cross-domain transfer: concepts from one course increasingly reappear without teacher prompting in another.

HARIL rule: every learning-pattern claim should expose its evidence, confidence and student response.
