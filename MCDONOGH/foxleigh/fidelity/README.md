## What is documented
The school's official 150 Stories #38 describes (1) a circa-1850 brick core with three stories, attic, full basement; (2) a frame addition extending east, with classrooms on floor one and student sleeping space on floor two; (3) a Trustees' Extension authorized in July 1873 further east, with dining room on the ground floor and classrooms above; (4) a rear L-shaped service arrangement; and (5) the mansion's destruction by fire on July 30, 1914.

## What the demo does
- Shows a differentiated three-story masonry core, eastern wood-frame addition, Trustees' Extension and simplified rear service zone
- Generates and repeats a single modelled sash-window assembly, with editable provisional pane count and inset depth
- Shows modeled sills, lintels, brickwork, porch and roof masses to explain the component-reuse method
- Offers orbit/focus camera, materials vs wireframe inspection, archival image reference links, screenshot and GLB export
- Works as an independent browser study without GPS, Google Maps API keys, or AR permissions

## Critical limits
This is **not photogrammetry**, a surveyed architectural record, or photo-calibrated reconstruction. The exact dimensions, window count, sash pattern, masonry bond, cornices, portico, roof pitch, chimneys, colors, interior plan, and wing proportions have not been confirmed and **must not be reported as facts**. External historic source images are referenced from McDonogh and must not be incorporated into commercial media or repo assets without rights review. GLB export is for the working hypothesis only. The browser-generated material textures are illustrative, not image-derived.

## Practical next pass
Choose one unoccluded window and the masonry around it. Perspective-rectify its best source scan, count visible panes, trace sill and lintel outlines, measure proportions relative to repeated brick courses, model only those established features, then render the same camera view to compare re-projection. Publish a feature-by-feature evidence manifest with confidence and image coordinates. Keep all nonvisible sides as geometrically modest, visually distinguished hypotheses until new archival evidence resolves them.
