# WARREN — FIELD STUDY 001 · EMBODIED SCENE

**Status:** playable historical-interpretation blockout. **Not** a georeferenced town or survey reconstruction.

**Primary entry point:** `https://www.matchzimmerman.com/WARREN/experience/` (once GitHub Pages deployment succeeds).

The viewer requires a WebGL1-capable browser but **does not require external JS libraries, map tiles, archival image servers, or generated textures**. The 3D scene is built from code. It can also run offline by opening `index.html` directly without additional files.

## Controls (participant-facing: keep minimal)

- Select **ENTER WARREN** to begin.
- Computer: `W/A/S/D` to move; mouse to look; Shift to move faster; Escape to release mouse, click scene to recapture.
- Touchscreen: left thumbstick moves, drag right portion of scene to look.
- `01 Street`, `02 Mill`, `03 River`: jump directly to three starting locations.
- `SOUND OFF/ON`: optional synthesized wind/water audio (off initially).
- `ⓘ EVIDENCE`: opens supporting research methods and links, optional and not required before encountering scene.

## What derives from actual history and what does not

**Historical anchors:** the river line, **three approximate road alignments** and **five rough building-symbol cluster extents** are transcribed from the Maryland State Archives' 1915 Bromley atlas image-space study. They preserve **relative relationships only**. The atlas is not yet georeferenced; there is no established meter-per-pixel or compass orientation. The code's meter scaling is a creative test setting, not a historic measured scale.

**Historically documented elements:** an industrial village with a **five-story textile mill** and workers' residences existed at Warren before demolition and Loch Raven inundation circa 1922. The five-story structure is supported by a 2008 Historical Society of Baltimore County account. Its precise position, building mass/dimensions, facade, adjacent smokestack and all other structures in this build are *provisional*, not archival reconstructions.

**Provisional environmental elements:** modeled hills are generated by a synthetic terrain function; **no actual surveyed pre-1922 valley elevations** are claimed. Modern lidar does not recover original submerged terrain. Bridge form, river width and depth, roads' widths, cottages' number and scale, trees, agricultural rows, figures, and time of day are creative hypotheses and do not assert historical accuracy.

**No AI image generation:** no generated images, sprites or textures. The world is constructed from procedural geometry and realtime WebGL shader with a limited palette, Bayer-style ordered dithering, toon quantization, fog and vegetation.

## Implementation / testing

- Static WebGL 1 triangles with custom buffer geometry; no NPM/runtime/library network dependency.
- A procedural hilly terrain surface, atlas-relative river, three roads, approximately 50–60 attempted cottage placements, one massed mill, a bridge, generated vegetation, six simple wandering human silhouettes.
- Moving camera uses position-relative terrain following, basic AABB house collision, mouse/pointerlock, WASD and touch virtual stick with right-side look.
- Optional synthesized wind/water generated locally with WebAudio and explicit opt-in. No audio recording or data collection.
- Tests: extraction of the inline script to `node --check`; compare hand-encoded road/river/cluster coordinate schema with `source-geometry.json` and archived annotation file. Browser rendering and target-device interaction must be separately tested; do not equate syntax checks with real browser acceptance.
