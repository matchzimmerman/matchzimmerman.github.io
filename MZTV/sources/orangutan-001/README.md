# MZTV · ORANGUTAN 001 · STUDIO  (G1 blockout, private branch)

**HARIL STUDIO #62** · MZ.GPT research → MZ.Claude build · source-locked to MZ.Human's instruction (Nov 22, 2026):
*"Use the asset studio from 48PLANT (same aesthetic, same MZCAM, same studio setup — just replace the plant with a 'live' animal."*

The 48 PLANT studio is reused (cyclorama, light, print pass, inspector, MZCAM crew), with the plant swapped for an original orangutan built entirely in code. No meshes, rigs, motion clips, character templates or generated images are loaded: anatomy, joints, coat, IK, attention and behaviour are constructed in `creature.js`.

**Status:** isolated on branch `mz-claude/orangutan-001`. Nothing here is linked from any public page, the 48 PLANT specimens/archive are untouched, and `main` is unchanged.

```
studio.html?seed=1            the animal in the 48 PLANT studio (inspector)
studio.html?seed=1&crew=1     the MZCAM four-drone crew films it, with monitors
studio.html?subject=test      a still lathe form: checks the studio shell with no animal
  &t=SEC   fast-forward the life before showing it     &rate=K   life runs K× faster
  &print=0 raw geometry    &wind=0 still coat    &bay=0 no monitors    &capture=1 frames advance only via MZ.frame(dt)
```
Keys: the original 48 PLANT set (drag, scroll/pinch, right-drag, double-click, WASD/QE, F, P, V, C, H) plus **B** crew on/off and **M** crew monitors.

### Correction to the #62 brief (verified in code)
The brief said a Three.js bridge for MZCAM would have to be built. One already exists in practice: **BIT FIELD 037 · SKATE GARDEN** and **039 · FLOWER FIELD** map the live drone's lens onto a `PerspectiveCamera` every frame and draw the drones as 3D meshes. It isn't a library API, but it's a proven pattern. ORANGUTAN 001 reuses it; `mzcam.js` is not modified. (48 PLANT's own inline crew draws in 2D canvas / raw WebGL, not Three.js.)

## The animal (`creature.js`)

Contract (new, as proposed in #62): `createCreature(THREE, {seed}) → { root, update(dt, t, wind), bounds(), observe(), reset(seed), log, metrics }`.

- **Anatomy:** adult female Bornean orangutan proportions (blockout). Native joint hierarchy (heading → pelvis → lumbar/chest → neck → head → eyes and lids). Limbs are two-bone chains solved each step: long arms (upper arm 0.37 m, forearm 0.35 m), short bowed legs, hook hands (four three-phalanx fingers plus a short thumb), hand-like feet (four curling toes plus an opposable, splayed hallux), pot belly, shoulder hump, high crown, dark facial disc, pale prognathic muzzle, small ears, chin beard. Scapulae slide a few cm toward far reaches.
- **Coat:** shaggy shells (hashed lobes keyed to position, so the surface stays watertight) plus about 450 instanced hanging locks. Each lock is a small pendulum that hangs by gravity, lags its anchor's motion, and feels the gusts. Crown hair stands up.
- **Behaviour (fixed 60 Hz step, seeded, deterministic):**
  - *sit:* breathes; shifts weight; repositions a hand; brings a hand up, turns the palm to its face and studies it while the fingers flex
  - *rise:* leans in, places one fist then the other, then lifts the body forward over planted feet
  - *quad stance*
  - *walk:* lateral-sequence fist-walking, with contacts planted in world space and placed for where the body will be at mid-stance (turning included)
  - *settle:* steps a limb that ended away from neutral, hind limbs first
  - *sit down:* drops back between planted feet, then places the hands
  - Breathing rate rises with effort and recovers.
- **Attention:** eyes saccade to a target first. The head follows after a 140–260 ms latency, the eyes counter-rotate as it arrives, the chest helps on big turns, and blinks ride on large shifts. Targets are the animal's own interests: the floor, the wall, up, its feet, its hand, the way ahead. **It never reads a camera.**
- **observe():** gaze, face, eyes, head, chest, pelvis, hands, feet, shoulders, planted contacts, state/activity and the hand being inspected. Cameras read this. Nothing writes back.

## Test procedure and results (2026-11-22)

1. `node tools/life-test.mjs 300 1,2,3,4,5` (needs `three@0.170.0` on the node path). Five seeds, 5 simulated minutes each. **All PASS:**
   - planted-contact slip ≤ 2e-16 m (zero skating)
   - peak reach 0.93–0.945 of limb length
   - largest pelvis move per 1/60 s step 7.3 mm (no pops)
   - eyes before head in 95–97% of gaze shifts, median lead 183–233 ms
   - every behaviour visited (5–9 rises, 5–13 walks per seed)
   - same seed ⇒ identical behaviour log
   - simulation ≈ 0.1 ms per step in Node
2. Headless Chrome (SwiftShader software GL) via Playwright, three.js served locally:
   - studio shell matches 48 PLANT side by side (see captures)
   - print on/off works
   - photograph downloads at 1920×1080 in print look, for both inspector and crew program
   - double-click flies to the hit point on the moving animal; F reframes to the moving bounds; drag and WASD work
   - B: the director cuts only to drones that are on their mark; monitors render through each drone's lens; drones appear in each other's shots
   - no page errors
3. Performance: in software GL, about 10 fps at 960×540 (render 4–10 ms/frame, simulation about 1.5 ms/frame). That is a lower bound, not a GPU figure. Raw-geometry mode: about 85 draw calls and 26k triangles. **Needs a measurement on a real GPU and OBS.**

## G1.1 · calibration from the #63 reference package v1 (MZ.GPT, 2026-11-22)
- **Limbs:** upper arm 0.325, forearm 0.328, thigh 0.253, shank 0.218 m. These are adult female *Pongo* group-mean bone lengths (Kralick et al. 2023), used as joint-centre proxies (Inferred). The arm-to-leg ratio barely moves (138.5 → 138.6). The trunk, neck and head are scaled 0.9 with the limbs, and contact layout, stance heights, travel and walk speed scale with limb length.
- **Gait:** now **diagonal-sequence** (hind foot, then the opposite hand), predominant in wild *P. pygmaeus* (Orlikoff et al. 2026). Phase offsets, duty factor and cycle time remain unpublished placeholders.
- **Hands:** each walking step is a fist about 88% of the time and a modified palm about 12% (43/6 of 49 contacts). The fist wrist is extended or neutral per step (19/23).
- **Feet:** inverted onto the outer edge when up, toes curled, heel leads into contact (34/37 heel strikes).
- **Still unsourced** (the package returned null): trunk, hand, foot and head dimensions; posture angles and contacts; gait timing; bout durations and transitions; blink, breathing and eye/head timing; hair lengths and colours; documentary holds.
- Life test after calibration: all 5 seeds pass; peak reach 0.94–0.96; slip ≈ 1e-16 m; peak limb speed 2.3 m/s.

## G1.2 · calibration from #63 package v2 (MZ.GPT, 2026-11-22)
- **Limbs:** now *P. pygmaeus*-specific female bone means, n = 15 (Powell et al. 2024): 0.335 / 0.338 / 0.26 / 0.23 m. v1's mixed-*Pongo* values are kept in a code comment.
- **Hands and feet:** hand 0.17 m and foot about 0.25 m, scaled from one adult male's external lengths (Isler et al. 2006) by the female/male humerus ratio (Inferred, single case).
- **Head and hips:** the head stays at G1 size, which matches two female skulls (zygomatic 0.133 m, max length 0.206 m). The seat widens to about 0.36 m, the external pelvic breadth of three captive females (Hammond et al. 2014).
- **Gait:** the bonobo duty-factor comparator (about 0.65) agrees with the 0.66 already in use; no change.
- **New behaviors (from the v2 ethogram distinctions):**
  - *scratching:* short (<5 s) or long (>5 s) bouts, to the head or to the opposite arm, belly or thigh; the hand oscillates at 3.5–5.5 Hz; gaze often elsewhere with an occasional glance
  - *fiddling:* fingers work on a resting hand while attention wanders
  - *engaged inspection:* the existing hand-to-face study with gaze locked
  - No dominant scratching hand is assumed.
- **Robustness:**
  - the walk eases its travel and turning when a planted limb nears full reach
  - the over-reach check runs before any state logic, and settling steps the most-strained limb first
  - getting up waits for all limbs to be still
- Life test: **10 seeds pass**; peak reach ≤ 0.986, slip ≈ 1e-16 m.

## Known gaps and errors (G1)
- **Missing biological references:** the proportions are estimates. Needed:
  - limb segment lengths and girths for adult female *Pongo pygmaeus*, and for the flanged male (cheek pads and throat sac are not modelled)
  - fist-walking vs palm-walking frequencies and hand postures
  - ground gait timing (duty factor, footfall sequence and speed)
  - sitting postures
  - hair length by body region
  - eye/head coordination latency in great apes
- Peak hand speed mid-swing is about 2.7 m/s, brisk for a slow terrestrial walk; swing timing needs reference footage.
- No bipedal standing yet ("stand" = rising to fists and feet). No arboreal or climbing behaviour, since the studio has nothing to climb.
- Locks don't collide with the body and can pass through a limb; the coat shells are rigid per segment, with no skinning at the joints.
- The hand held up for inspection can sit close to the muzzle from some angles.
- The crew's shot timing hasn't been tuned against 48 PLANT's documentary rhythm (no 30-second rule, no "never two close-ups in a row").
- Monitor labels are HTML over the canvas (as in 48 PLANT's bay), not in the dithered frame.
