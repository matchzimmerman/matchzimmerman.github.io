# ONE EDIT
## MZ WORLDS / EXPERIMENT 001
### Creative brief · October 24, 2026

**Form:** Browser-native, audiovisual, living-systems artwork. **Not** a textbook, tutorial, predictive biosimulator, or biological editing service.

**Question:** What does it feel like to alter an inherited instruction in a living system while being unable to dictate its eventual expression and consequences?

### Visitor encounter
Two simultaneously evolving, initially matched fields are visible: a control field and an intervention field. The visitor observes, chooses the intervention site and the extent of contact, and performs one modeled genetic intervention. Descendants then inherit the changed state. The visitor may also alter the external adenine environment in both fields, discovering that a biological difference can persist even when the visible phenotype becomes muted.

### Actual biological anchor
**Species:** *Saccharomyces cerevisiae*, budding yeast.  
**Locus:** ADE2, part of the adenine biosynthetic pathway.  
**Manipulation:** CRISPR-Cas9 disruption of ADE2; and a distinct repair/restoration scenario. The actual technology can do both, but this art model abstracts the repair/editing process as an outcome event.  
**Observable relationship:** Disrupted ADE2 can generate red pigmentation on adenine-limited medium; the same mutation can be less visibly red when adenine is abundant. Disruption compromises adenine biosynthesis, making growth dependent on available adenine.

**Evidence:** Shortt, Krippaehne & Wasko (2023), *A simple and accessible CRISPR genome editing laboratory exercise using yeast*: https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC9941855/ ; 2021 lab module: https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC8442027/ ; CRI-SPA (2023), culture-dependent phenotype: https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC10516668/ ; yeast genome editing and restoration (2018): https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/30311729/.

### The experiential grammar
**Observe → Locate → Intervene → Inherit → Recondition → Compare.**

The work avoids quiz questions, achievement loops, invented exact probabilities, tutorial arrows, and presenting technical mechanisms as aesthetic spectacle. It allows a visitor to become uncertain whether the edit “worked”—and then to inspect two different kinds of evidence: genotype and appearance.

### Ethical and epistemic boundaries
- Only nonphysical, simulated organisms are present. No DNA sequences, guide RNAs, laboratory conditions/protocols or wet-lab operating instructions are generated.
- The modeled probabilities, growth multipliers, time units, color intensities, spatial motion and sonic mapping are **invented art parameters**, never real empirical predictions.
- The limited ADE2 system is presented as **one evidence-anchored example**, not as a stand-in for every genome-editing method, species or gene.
- Off-target editing, chromosomal complexity, individual alleles, repair-pathway choice, real biochemical kinetics, dormancy and environmental transport are explicitly out of scope for this prototype.
- The sonic score is procedural and responds to simulation state; it does not reproduce measurements from an actual experiment.

### Success test
A visitor should be able to notice that (1) a genetic change is not automatically a visible change, (2) descendants can carry the change, (3) environmental conditions alter expression and population dynamics, and (4) an untouched control helps separate intervention from ordinary drift. None of these observations should require reading a lesson.
