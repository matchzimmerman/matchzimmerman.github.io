# MZTV · BIT FIELD 041 · MZ WORLD · WHITE CUBE

One sculpture, one vacuum, many observers. MZ.Human's assemblage sculpture stands alone in an empty white room: a stack of guitar amps on a pallet with a stopped clock, two screens, a Kinect, weathered boards, a crown of copper wire and headphones, and an open case of red wiring. Seven floating MZ CAMs observe it, compose it and broadcast it. Nothing else is in the room. It is printed every frame as carved lines in six inks on HEADLAND's engine, with SRUTHÁN v3's surface-anchored hatching and 48 PLANT's camera crew.

## Source evidence, and what is inferred
- **Reconstructed from:** MZ.Human's four photographs of the pallet version (front-left, wide, three-quarter, close three-quarter) and the walk-around video `IMG_2424.mov` (27 s). All were attached to the MZ.Claude conversation on 2026-11-05. They are not in either repository.
- **Not used:** the six photographs of the earlier arrangement, which has no pallet, the Bugera on top, rope, and the clock lying in the case. MZ.Human chose the pallet version. The two images generated by MZ.GPT were also not used; they are the rejected aesthetic.
- **Measured from the photos:** each part's position, layering and relative size, read against the Fender Twin (about 66 × 51 cm). Every size is an estimate.
- **Merged from two moments:** the photos show the screens off. The video shows the piece running: the iMac shows a warm field, and the ViewSonic shows a line figure from the Kinect. The video also shows the Kinect on the Line 6 and the MG10 chassis moved elsewhere. This world keeps the chassis where the photos have it, puts the Kinect behind it, and runs the screens as the video shows them.
- **Inferred, not photographed:** the backs of the amps, the iMac's stand and back, the white cabinet under the iMac (one shows in the video), and the undersides.
- **Left blank on purpose:** brand logos are blank chrome plates, and the Bob Ross card is a blank pink tag. The clock's numerals are typeset in a plain sans, not Timex's face.
- **Documentary detail:** the Timex is stopped. It reads 8:55:15 in every photograph and in the video, with the second hand in the same place each time. The world keeps it stopped.

## Deliberate breaks from the HEADLAND standard
- **No place and no weather.** Rule 1 asks for a real place with live weather. This world is a vacuum: the white cube is not located, and nothing outside it gets in. The info box says so. Real time is used only to timestamp the log.
- **The room has no edges.** The floor meets the walls in shade, not in an outline, and the shade fades with distance, so the cube dissolves into unprinted paper. This is what "vacuum" means here: the paper is the void.
- **Inhabitants are the observers.** There is no figure with errands. The seven drones and the Kinect are the life of the place, and the sculpture is the subject.
- **The bass is what the lens looks at.** Rule 9 says the bass degree comes from where the central figure is. Here every amp hums on its own degree, and the amp (or part) under the live lens becomes the bass.

## World
- **The white cube:** 12 × 12 m, 5.4 m high. The sculpture stands just off centre. Light comes from one key above and a high ambient, as in a white room. The key direction changes only at rupture.
- **The sculpture:**
  - On a weathered pallet (top boards running front to back): a Fender Twin and a lower Fender combo.
  - On those: an Ibanez bass amp, a Fender Frontman 15G and a Fender Champ 12 with its handle.
  - On top: a Marshall MG10, and a Line 6 Spider lying on its side so its control strip runs up the right.
  - The MG10CD chassis out of its cabinet, gold faceplate forward with red tape across it.
  - A Kinect.
  - Top left: a small speaker box with a crown of copper wire, three pairs of headphones, two loose drivers, the tag, a black clamp and a pipe-cleaner ornament.
  - Behind the stack: an iMac on a white cabinet, and four weathered pickets with nails down their edges.
  - Leaning on the Champ: the white ViewSonic panel.
  - On the pallet front: a cassette receiver on a silver VCR, with the clock leaning against the amps above them.
  - On the floor front right: the open case, the circuit board in its base and the lid standing up.
  - Wiring: a red wire bundle spilling across the pallet, a white cord from the Champ down to the case, black leads, and the Kinect's red lead.
- **The Kinect** only tilts, as the real one does: ±27° at about 12°/s, with a motor whirr. It follows the nearest drone in front of it.
- **The screens:** the ViewSonic shows the Kinect's view as a line sketch, with drones drawn bright and the Kinect's speckle inside them. The iMac shows a warm field.

## Life (the crew is the life of the room)
- **Seven MZ CAMs** on the shared `MZTV/lib/mzcam.js`, the same crew as 48 PLANT, TRACKSIDE and SITTER, so the flight model is not duplicated:
  - Drones fly to their marks off air, hold still on air, and slide out of the live lens while travelling.
  - They keep out of the sculpture's envelope and out of each other's way.
  - Each one is a printed cube with a lens barrel and a tally light that prints in HOT when it is live. They float (there is no air to push) and cast printed shadows on the paper floor. They appear in each other's shots.
- **Roles:**

  | Role | What it does |
  |---|---|
  | OBSERVER | wide, 4–5 m out, attentive to the empty room |
  | ORBIT | one slow revolution every 4 minutes |
  | DETAIL | 0.5–0.95 m from a part |
  | SIGNAL | the electronics: Kinect, screens, chassis, board, cassette, VCR, clock |
  | DRIFT | wandering, aimed at the least-attended part |
  | LOW | floor level, looking up |
  | TOP | from under the ceiling |

- **Curiosity:** each drone plans its next shot from what the crew has looked at least. Parts that have been looked at less are wanted more.

## Memory
- **Attention:** every part keeps the seconds the crew has spent on it, weighted by distance and three times for the live lens. After 7 weighted minutes a part becomes *known*, and its close-ups move off-centre onto sub-parts. First looks are logged ("CAM 3 took the first close look at Fender Champ 12").
- **Floor scuffs:** where drones hover low, the paper floor wears. It is a 48 × 48 grid over the room that builds over hours and never recovers.
- **The clock:** it stays stopped. Whenever it runs (see below), it keeps the time it reached, so over the world's life the dial creeps forward from 8:55:15, only while watched.
- **Events:** each event remembers the hour, the part under the lens and how many lenses were watching, and recurs more readily under similar conditions. Repeats habituate.
- **Saving:** state lives in `localStorage` under `mzworld-white-cube-v1`, saved every 60 s and on unload. An OBS reload resumes the same epoch, attention, scuffs, clock and log.

## Cameras
The director cuts only to a drone that is on its mark and ready, holds each shot 8–19 s, and cuts on the 16th note of the bar. A shot can interrupt only if it is wanted much more. Wanting follows each part's novelty and avoids repeating the same role, and during an event it favours drones that can see the event's subject. `?cam=N` holds one drone; **C** shows the studio plan, with each drone, its cone and the tally on the live one.

## The uncanny
| Event | Conditions | What happens |
|---|---|---|
| The stack fed back | a lens within 0.78 m of a speaker's front, facing it | HOT rings radiate across that amp's grille cloth, the grilles shiver, and the amp's hum rises with a howl on its own degree. The ♭II chord and a reverse swell come in. The crew flinches: the envelope it keeps out of grows by 0.5 m and every drone backs off |
| The screens showed the crew to itself | 5 or more of the 7 lenses on the piece at once | the iMac shows a drone's live feed, printed in the same inks: a print inside the print. A high fifth in the room |
| The clock ran | the clock on air within 2.6 m, with nothing else happening | the stopped Timex starts and ticks for 50–140 s, then stops again where it got to. A sub swells with a breath |
| Hush | tension reaches 1 | the drones stop mid-air, the hums drop away, the Kinect holds; then rupture |

Each event has its own cooldown, and the global gap is 14–34 min.

## Rupture
Tension builds with stability (epochs of about 50–95 min) and with each event (+0.08). At 1 the room holds still. Then:
- the print re-inks with a new colourway (weighted toward the least visited) and a new line grammar (period, dither, wobble, outlines, hatch);
- the cube is relit from a new direction, so the contact shadow and every drone's shadow move;
- the harmonic centre moves, and the hums retune over several seconds.

**Colourways:** Tolex (home), Timex, Pallet, Cassette (new); Signal Flare, Pistachio Night, Ultramarine, Lichen (shared).

## Print
- **Data target:** the scene renders to a data target (shade, material id, carved line value, accent), and the print pass resolves it to six inks with ordered dither, speckle and outlines between materials.
- **Hatching** is carved into the surfaces and spaced in octaves with distance (SRUTHÁN v3):
  - grille cloth is a woven pitch (5.5 mm) that steps out with distance;
  - wood runs along the grain, with nail heads down the pickets' edges;
  - tolex is a fine diagonal.
- **New here, a print inside the print:** the ViewSonic and the iMac show other lenses (the Kinect's and a drone's). They are rendered into their own data targets and printed in the same inks.
- **New here, floating shadows:** the drones cast soft printed shadows on the floor, offset by the key light. The sculpture's contact shadow follows the footprint of the pallet, the case and the cabinet.

## Sound (the live lens is the microphone)
D phrygian family; the centre moves only at rupture.
- **The amps:** every amplifier hums on its own degree, with a soft second harmonic, placed and weighted by the lens. The part under the live lens is the bass, held, with slow glides between cuts and no walking line.
  - Twin = I, Marshall = ♭II, Champ = ♭III, Frontman = IV, lower Fender = V, Ibanez = ♭VI, Line 6 = ♭VII.
- **Tempo:** a second hum sits just above the Twin's, and the beat between them (0.15–0.25 Hz) is the bar (4.0–6.6 s). It drifts as the amps warm and settle. It sets the echo and quantises the cuts; nothing plays on the beat.
- **Small sounds:**
  - room tone: a long, dim convolution for a big empty cube
  - a dry low hover when another drone is near the live lens
  - crackles from the circuit board when the lens is near the case
  - tuned plinks near the headphones
  - the Kinect's tilt motor
  - now and then a soft pad with tape wow on it, as from the cassette receiver
- **Events:** ♭II chord, howl and reverse swell (feedback); high fifth in the room (screens); clock ticks and a sub with a breath (clock).
- **Dub echo:** a unity-gain tanh loop whose feedback follows how many lenses are on the piece.
- **Master:** HP 26 Hz → tanh → high shelf −10 dB @ 2 kHz → LP 6.8 kHz → glue compressor.

## Hardening
- Noise comes from an AudioWorklet (data: URL, blob fallback, long buffers as a last resort).
- One-shots are enveloped and self-disconnect, and their queue is bounded.
- The lookahead scheduler skips forward after stalls.
- A watchdog runs every 1 s: it resumes a suspended context, and rebuilds on close, a clock stall over 6 s, NaN or 20 s of silence. It also refreshes every 3 h.
- Drone positions, tension, attention and the clock self-heal from non-finite values.
- WebGL context loss is handled.
- The world is pre-warmed for 65 s, so the drones are already on their marks and one is on air.
- Phones (a screen under 700 px on its short side) default to 480 lines.

## Tested
Run on 2026-11-05 in headless Chromium with SwiftShader (software WebGL). Stills from these runs are in `stills/`.

- **Renders:** all seven drones rendered at 1920×1080, plus photo-matched front, right and back views. Each event was forced and rendered, along with a rupture into the PALLET inks. No console errors.
- **10 simulated hours (seed 7):**
  - 30 events (screens, feedback, clock), about one every 20 min
  - 10 ruptures, about hourly
  - no NaN
  - 1,626 cuts in the first 6 hours, held 8–19 s (mean 13.3 s); longer holds only during the hush
- **Parts known by hour:** 0, 10, 17, 21, 22, then all 23 by hour 6. The clock ran six times in six hours and moved from 8:55:15 to 9:03:14.
- **Resume:** a reload (without `?fresh`) kept the epoch, the inks, the clock's position, attention and the log.
- **WebGL:** context loss and restore recovered and drew the next frame.
- **Audio recovery:** a suspended AudioContext resumed, a closed one was rebuilt, and poisoning tension and attention with NaN healed on the next frame.
- **16 s recordings of the master** (bands relative to 60–120 Hz):

  | Condition | RMS | Peak | 30–60 Hz | 2–5 kHz | above 5 kHz |
  |---|---|---|---|---|---|
  | quiet | −18.0 dB | −7.0 dBFS | −25 dB | −86 dB | −108 dB |
  | the stack fed back | −16.2 dB | −4.9 dBFS | −26 dB | −48 dB | −65 dB |
  | the clock ran | −18.0 dB | −5.2 dBFS | −5 dB | −56 dB | −81 dB |
  | the screens woke | −19.3 dB | −8.0 dBFS | −22 dB | −56 dB | −80 dB |

  - 60–120 Hz was the loudest band in every case.
  - No clicks: the only window above 5 kHz louder than −60 dBFS was the recorder starting at t = 0.
  - The clock's sub was turned down after a first recording put 30–60 Hz 4 dB above 60–120 Hz.
- **Load, same machine, 640×360, `?lines=240`, software rendering:** WHITE CUBE ran at 5.7 fps, HEADLAND at 2.3 and OUTBUILDING at 1.0. That makes it the lightest of the three, but these are software numbers; it has not been measured on a GPU.

## Known limits
- It has only been rendered in software (SwiftShader). GPU load on the streaming machine has not been measured; use `?lines=540` or `?lines=420` if it struggles. Each frame also renders the Kinect's view (96 × 72, every third frame) and, during the screens event, a drone feed (192 × 120).
- The reconstruction is by eye from four photographs and a video. It has not been measured against the sculpture. Small parts (the copper crown, the ornament, the wiring) are composed to match the photos' density and silhouette, not wire for wire.
- The backs are inferred.
- The experimental mode the brief mentions (procedural screens and sound beyond what the video shows) is not built. The only extensions beyond the documentary are the three events, each logged as an event.
