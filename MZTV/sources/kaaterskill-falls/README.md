An explorable, autonomous-camera six-ink landscape focused on Kaaterskill Falls and the stone staircase connecting its tiers. An **independent MZ World** informed by HEADLAND's principles (3D world, materially legible print, wandering/observer modes, external environmental input), **not a replacement for HEADLAND**. It does not borrow or alter the original HEADLAND scene, inhabitants, state key or custom shaders.

## What is running in Study 001

- A single **procedural 3D gorge** with dynamically shaded trees, bedrock layers, boulders, cliff faces and creek bed. Local world coordinates are approximate **metres**.
- Two continuous animated sheets of water, an upstream creek, a middle pool and a downstream basin. Upper water sheet 53 m, lower 26 m — a modeled 79 m total, corresponding to the reported 175 ft and 85 ft drops.
- **200 individual three-dimensional stone treads** on a descending switchback path, plus an upper forest connector, a middle-pool spur and the upper overlook/platform. Terrain corridors are carved toward the path lines for prototype walkability.
- Independent MZCAM-like views from the cliff, overlook, middle pool, staircase, downstream basin and aerial gorge. Camera cuts every ~14 seconds. No pre-rendered backgrounds.
- Visitor **WALK** third-person mode, **FLY** free-camera mode, waypoint jumps and an overhead field survey based on the same coordinate/height function as the world.
- MZ/HEADLAND-inspired screen-space six-ink print treatment and moving-water shader, no generated pictures/assets.
- The current Open-Meteo weather at the falls, refreshed at 15-minute intervals when accessible, influences light, haze, wind and *illustrative* flow. Autogenerated waterfall broadband ambience after a voluntary sound activation.
- Local no-storage reproducible terrain. A capture API: `MZ.tick(dt)`, `MZ.observe(index)`, `MZ.walk()`, `MZ.fly()`, `MZ.jump(index)`, `MZ.height(x,z)`, `MZ.ink(on)`, `MZ.weather()`, `MZ.site`, `MZ.routes`, `MZ.stairs`, `MZ.state`.

## Control surface

WASD / arrows — walk; mouse drag — turn view; Shift — run.

**V** WALK / OBSERVE; **C** next shot; **F** enter/leave fly camera; **SPACE** ascend and **Q** descend in flight; **M** on/off generated sound; **1–5** jump to documented landmarks; **? / H** help.

Touch: left thumbstick moves, drag right side to orbit. Overhead survey landmarks are clickable/tappable.

Render variants: `?ink=0` raw shaded materials, `?cam=0..5` fixed camera, `?lines=460` print pass internal resolution, `?capture=1` frame-step mode.

## Data basis / accuracy boundary

**Evidence-backed anchors**, obtained from public agency documentation:

1. Kaaterskill Falls is a two-tier waterfall on **Spruce Creek** in Greene County, New York; a **260-foot drop** with **175-foot upper** and **85-foot lower** sections ([NYSDEC trail guidance](https://dec.ny.gov/things-to-do/hiking/catskill-hikes)).
2. A **roughly 260-foot-long, 200-step stone staircase** was constructed in 2016 as a designated route near the falls, alongside an upper trail connection, a spur to the middle pool, and the accessible upper overlook ([NYSDEC 2015 release](https://apps.cio.ny.gov/apps/mediacontact/public/view.cfm?backButton=&parm=EE01E2F4-5056-9D0B-1A3A5642B3B60939); [NYSDEC 2017 safety account](https://content.govdelivery.com/accounts/NYSDEC/bulletins/1b6d5ff)).
3. From Laurel House Road, the **upper observation platform is approximately 0.3 miles**; an approximately **0.7-mile** trail descends toward the lower pools ([NYSDEC Kaaterskill Wild Forest](https://dec.ny.gov/places/kaaterskill-wild-forest)).
4. The gorge exposes sedimentary rock layers associated with **Devonian sandstones, shales and conglomerates** ([New York State Museum geology](https://www.nysm.nysed.gov/research-collections/geology/resources/kaaterskill)).
5. Open-Meteo conditions are a **weather proxy** for atmospheric behavior. They do not constitute real-time Spruce Creek water discharge.

**Not yet surveyed or claimed accurate:** the terrain heightfield, actual cliff coordinates, outline and switchback placement, tree inventory, steps' exact locations/heights, hydraulic shape, real-time water discharge, and trail accessibility. The map is for exploratory navigation **inside the artwork only**; it is not a hiking map or a safety guide. The waterfall is a hazardous real place. Stay on designated trails in person.

## Daylight + current-date foliage (2026-10-23 update)

- **Daylight only:** the environment uses an approximately 1 PM *seasonally adjusted* solar angle at Kaaterskill's latitude. The simulated camera never transitions to night. Current cloud/rain reports still influence brightness and haze; clock time does not dim the world.
- **Actual New York calendar:** `scene.js` calculates the current **America/New_York** calendar day (including leap years). Spring leaf-out, full summer canopy, autumn color/leaf drop and winter bare branches follow a continuous approximate seasonal curve. Trees, ground colors and leaf-litter cover derive from this state. **A 48-hour run refreshes tree foliage automatically within two minutes of an NY midnight.**
- **Late October 2026 is past peak, not peak foliage:** the October 21–27 NY report places **Hunter** in Greene County at *100% changed, past-peak* with mostly muted yellow/orange and significant leaf drop. This scene therefore keeps bare deciduous branches, remaining ochre/rust crowns, evergreen conifers and fallen leaves rather than an unrealistically full green/yellow canopy. The forest includes early-shedding exposed crowns and better-preserved sheltered valley crowns.
- **Source, contemporary 2026 observation:** [I LOVE NY Fall Foliage Report — Week of October 21–27](https://www.iloveny.com/things-to-do/fall/foliage-report/), with [October 22 Greene County/Hunter summary](https://www.aol.com/articles/ny-fall-foliage-report-regions-121010000.html).
- **Scope:** foliage is **date-aligned and calibrated to a reported regional condition**, not remotely sensed daily foliage at individual trees. The scene does not pull a foliage service continuously. Weather remains an independently fetched environmental input. The deciduous tree positions/species and terrain are still an interpretive reconstruction.

Validation: source modules pass JavaScript syntax inspection; mobile Safari/WebGL scene and long-running calendar rollover remain unverified until browser testing.
