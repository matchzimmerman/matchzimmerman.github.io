=== rg04 | M | 2026-10-05 | rg | verbatim
Flashing. The waves now move under 0.6 cycles per second; v1 reached about 6. Every pixel's brightness is also rate-limited, so a full dark-to-light swing takes at least 0.75 s. I measured it under far more impulses than the real data produces: no area can flicker anywhere near the 3-per-second photosensitivity threshold. Impulse rings are lower-contrast and spread slowly.
Nodes. Each player now emerges at a spot and stays tethered there. Its tether shows as a faint dashed circle. The player wanders inside it and gets shoved by its own hits, so you see the groove in the motion. Players that fall silent re-emerge near the newest wounds, which is where they meet the others. Collisions are permanent: the two players trade a beat, their timing locks closer together, their tethers tighten, each gains a ring, so you can read a node's collision history at a glance.
Funk. The drone is gone. The five nodes are now a band: bass, kick, snare, clav and horn-style stab. They play an interlocking, swung 16th-note groove in G dorian, with each player sitting slightly ahead of or behind the beat. Each impulse: the band answers with a stop-time hit on the next one, then a bar of just echoes. Most of that heals over the next bars, and some never does. Pressure drops the band into a breakdown. Spent ground: a player there falls silent. If it never finds intact ground again, it's gone for good. Highs rolled off further.
"Dead" is gone from the info box, the log and the README.

=== rg08 | M | 2026-10-06 | rg | doc
v4 is slower and more composed, with a crafted stereo and frequency field. About 52 bpm, half-time, drifting over 90 minutes and dragged slower by pressure and residue. Tonal center D phrygian. The sections are vamps inside the mode. The bass line is chosen from chord tones, so every note stays in key. Bass and kick dead center; rim slightly right; skank right; horn left; low strings hard left; high strings hard right. Two continuous string sections glide legato between voicings using nearest-note voice leading and swell on 32-bar arcs. They are the membrane: each side's level and brightness follow wave energy on that half of the field. More space means more strings. The lead rotates on 16-bar phrases between horn, strings, bass and skank. Everything else sits on a bed bus whose gain dips under each lead note. Master: highpass, light saturation, presence dip at 3 kHz, high shelf down, lowpass, glue compressor. Full range with a soft top.

=== rg11 | M | 2026-10-06 | rg | doc
The dub echo's feedback loop had a saturator with about 1.9 times small-signal gain. Once feedback passed about 0.53, which dub-outs and high pressure reached, the loop gain exceeded 1 and the echo self-oscillated into a sustained low roar. It was bounded but did not decay. The loop now uses a unity-gain soft clip, so loop gain equals the feedback value and every echo decays. Patched in v1 through v5. It's fixed, so every echo now decays.

=== rg17 | M | 2026-10-06 | rg | doc
SCORE ENGINE 002 composes to a clip's exact length: it plans a six-section form whose energy belongs to the composition, and treats the footage as an arbitrary trigger source. What the footage controls changes from section to section. The footage never sets the energy. A dull stretch of B-roll in the Climax still drives saturation, octave horn stabs and dub-outs, because those are what the Climax routes it to. The footage works like a humanizer or shuffle: it adds unplanned timing and variation inside a composed structure. Archival B-roll suits it well, because the same shot taken again and again gives you repetition with variation. Everything is deterministic. The same clip with the same seed gives the same piece.
