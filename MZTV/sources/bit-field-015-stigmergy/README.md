# BIT FIELD 015 — STIGMERGY

Tens of thousands of agents on a torus lay trail and follow trail. Transport networks form out of that feedback alone, with no plan and no centre. The camera looks down through three stacked planes:

- **live trail** (top): the network as it is right now
- **channels** (middle): what the trail has worn, built up over minutes and fading over an hour
- **sediment** (bottom): what the channels have held, built up over tens of minutes and fading over hours

Parallax between the planes gives the depth, and low light casts the veins' shadows down onto the planes below. Memory changes what becomes possible: agents are drawn back into old channels, reinforcement thickens worn routes into trunks, and the sediment's strongest old hubs come back as food the network reorganises around.

A packet rides the strongest vein, and the camera follows it. The sound is what the packet meets. The vein under it is the sub-bass line: worn trunks sit on the root and thin new veins lean on the flat second. A vein entering its earshot is a pluck, placed and ringed where it appears, and a junction it crosses is an accent. Ground coverage drives the kick, loops in the network extend the chords, and channel depth feeds the echo. Home key is D Phrygian.

The rules mutate over hours. When the network settles, saturates or starves, or the packet keeps retracing worn channels, pressure builds. The genome then deforms over about three minutes into a new regime (network, meander, knots, streams, cells), with rules unlocked from memory: reinforcement, food from the sediment, a second colony with its own packet and counter-voice, and cuts that wound the ground and heal. State persists across reloads.
