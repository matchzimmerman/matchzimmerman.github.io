# MZTV · BIT FIELD 019 · IF THE BIRDS ARE OUT

A poem by Match Zimmerman, set as an OBAS field. The film runs 2:17. Each stanza is one shot, and each line lands on the beat (82 bpm) with no fades.

Everything happens in one world: a house across the street, a front window and a back window that slide into alignment, and the ocean through both.

1. **House.** The windows align, and the shades come down on "When pulled shut."
2. **Night.** Shadows slip across the room, the lamps glow, and the camera pushes in on a lamp base full of shells.
3. **Sunrise.** The camera goes through the glass. Pelicans cross ahead of a peeling wave that spirals onto shore, and crabs sprint sideways into dry holes and spy.
4. **Chop.** The birds aren't out. Crossing swell and undercurrent swirls appear, the shoreline dances, and the horizon drops for "endless expanse."
5. **Foam.** Frayed foam fibers let go of the sand. Then sun and moon cycle, day and night, as the tide runs low and high.
6. **Tonight.** The shades are shut. Sunrise lifts them for the birds, and the view pulls back to the narrator's own bedside window.

Each shot has its own four-colour palette and dither grammar (bayer, pearl, bayer 4, grain), so every cut is a rupture.

**Sound.** One score, rendered from the same script as the image:
- surf that swells every two bars, in step with the visual swash
- pad chords that change on the cut
- dub bass with a one-drop kick and filtered stabs into a dotted-eighth delay
- a voice for each of the poem's events: windows align into unison, the room goes muffled behind the shade, lamp tones and rattling shells, pelican glides, a reverse swell into the curl, a panned run of crab knocks, a wobbling undercurrent, reverse-attack foam, and a D major lift on "If the birds are out"

Everything sits under about 1 kHz, and nothing is above 5 kHz.
