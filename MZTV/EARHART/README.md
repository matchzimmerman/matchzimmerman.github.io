# MZTV / EARHART

Pieces made with MZ.Claude around Amelia Earhart and Fred Noonan's last flight, 2 July 1937. Each folder is a self-contained web page.

| Piece | What it is |
|---|---|
| [`north-and-south/`](north-and-south/) | Fly the dawn approach to Howland from the cockpit, into the computed sunrise, looking for a two-kilometre island and sending her logged messages into silence. Each flight starts somewhere different, because nobody knows where she was. |
| [`no-reference-point/`](no-reference-point/) | A navigator's plotting sheet for the morning: the last messages as the Navy recorded them twice, and why the line of position ran 157–337 (a sunrise sun line, computed). |
| [`the-last-flight-air/`](the-last-flight-air/) | The MZTV experience edition: a printed Pacific world flying the newest tested model version from the research engine, with a versioned archive of every edition (`archive/`). |
| [`tuned-to-3105/`](tuned-to-3105/) | Classroom study: run the Itasca's radio room. Kept as a record; superseded by North and South. |
| [`line-of-position/`](line-of-position/) | Classroom study: a teacher-led room-scale lesson. Kept as a record; superseded by North and South. |

**Sources.** Quotes are from US Navy, *Report of Earhart Search by U.S. Navy and U.S. Coast Guard, July 2–18, 1937* (National Archives Earhart release, file 080_PC31-22_146_07, pages stamped NW 90261), checked against the page images. Sun positions are computed for Howland on 2 July 1937 (sunrise 17:46 GCT, bearing 66.9°; the report gives 1745). Clouds, sea, sound, camera work and the start position in North and South are authored. Nothing here claims where the airplane went.

**Research engine.** The ingest, ledger and model-versioning work (BUILD-001) lives on branch `claude/last-flight-build-001` under `MZTV/sources/earhart-last-flight-research-engine/`. The original public prototype is `MZTV/sources/earhart-last-flight/`.
